Front-end & combat HUD

Every screen between opening the URL and throwing the first punch, plus the HUD that has to stay legible while two fighters and four effects fight over the same pixels.

Law 1 · pad firstNothing requires a mouse. One item is always focused, one button always confirms, one button always goes back.
Law 2 · three pressesTitle to fighting in three inputs. The menu's job is to get out of the way.
Law 3 · genre firstThe HUD spends zero novelty. Angled bars, portraits, centre plate — legible on sight to anyone who has held a pad. Save invention for the menus.

Two players, one machine. Best of three rounds. Player one takes the first connected pad; player two takes the second, or the keyboard.

Local 2P sits first because it is the mode that convinces people. The demo is two humans laughing at a desk, not one person versus a bot.

Screen 06 · the important one

Combat HUD

Genre-standard architecture: angled portrait, gold health bar, meter beneath, hexagonal timer plate between them. Familiar on sight to anyone who has played a fighting game, which is the point — the HUD is the one place to spend zero novelty.

Angled, not curved. Bars are parallelograms cut with clip-path, leaning outward from centre. Health is a shared gold gradient on both sides — gold reads as life to anyone who has held a pad, and player identity is carried by side, portrait accent and the P1/P2 tag instead.

The red ghost is the whole point of the bar. Damage animates in 420ms; a red layer behind it catches up over 1s after a 300ms delay, so the player sees exactly what a combo cost after it has already ended.

Second bar is Ultimate charge, not a meter system — the slice has no Circuit. It fills as health drops and turns gold when the ultimate is available. When Circuit ships post-slice it takes this same slot with no layout change.

Hexagonal plate anchors the centre. Timer at 6.2cqw with tabular figures, round label beneath, two pips per player flanking the base. Timer turns Cinder at ten seconds — colour and a glow change, never colour alone.

Combo counter is right-aligned under the HUD, damage in italic below the count. It appears at hit two, not hit one, and sits clear of the lower two thirds where the fight actually is.

Portraits are 8cqw angled quads with a coloured base stripe — blue for P1, gold for P2. In the slice they carry a display initial; the real build swaps in a rendered bust.

Scrim is 82% opaque, not blurred. Blur costs a fullscreen pass; the fight is meant to be visible-but-suspended, and opacity does that for free.

Behaviour

Interaction model

The rules that make a pad-first UI feel right, and the ones that most often get skipped.

RuleSpec
FocusExactly one focused element per screen at all times. Focus survives back-navigation — returning to the menu restores the item you left from.
Repeat rateFirst step on press, then 24-frame delay, then one step per 8 frames. Faster feels twitchy on a stick; slower feels broken.
WrappingVertical lists wrap. Horizontal card rows do not — hitting the end should feel like an edge.
Confirm / backA confirms and B backs out on every screen without exception, including inside the pause menu.
MouseSupported as a convenience: click sets focus and confirms in one action. Nothing is mouse-only, and no hover-only state exists.
Transitions120ms cross-fade between screens. No slides, no scale, no easing flourishes — menu latency is felt.
AudioNavigation and confirm sounds fire on input, before the visual updates. A menu that sounds late feels late.
DisconnectPad unplugged mid-match pauses instantly and names the missing device. Never silently swap to keyboard.
Constraints

Accessibility & legibility

  • Never colour alone. P1/P2 carry blue/gold and left/right position and a printed tag. Low health is red and a pulsing arc. Ready is solid and says "Ready".
  • Blue/gold survives all three common colour-vision types — the pairing was chosen over the obvious red/blue for exactly this reason.
  • Minimum type size in the HUD is 1.05cqw (≈13px at 1280 wide). Anything smaller is decoration and gets cut.
  • Reduced motion stops the title prompt pulse, the ultimate pill pulse, and all screen transitions. Health-bar animation stays — it carries information.
  • Contrast: all HUD text ≥ 7:1 against arena ink. Stone on ink is 5.4:1 and is used only for labels, never for values.
  • Keyboard-only players can reach and operate every screen; the slice is playable start to finish with no pad attached.

Implementation notes

For the agent building the slice. This adds one item to scope — F21 — and does not change anything else.

ItemNote
Where it livesAll menu screens are DOM overlays in src/ui/, not rendered in the R3F canvas. Only the HUD sits over the canvas, still as DOM.
StateScreen routing and menu focus go in the zustand UI store. The HUD reads health and timer from the sim through a throttled bridge at 15Hz — never a per-frame React update.
ScalingScreens use container-type: inline-size and cqw units, exactly as these mockups do. The layout then holds at any window size without a media query.
Health arcsInline SVG path, stroke-dasharray driven by pathLength. Two stacked paths: red ghost behind, coloured fill in front with a shorter transition.
FontsSystem stacks only. Impact and Haettenschweiler cover Windows and macOS; the Arial Narrow Bold fallback covers the rest. No webfont, no CDN.
Build orderTitle, menu and setup during T-05. HUD during T-05. Pause, result and controls during T-09. Do not build these before combat feels right.
